11+ Time Management During the Exam

Time is your biggest enemy in the 11+ exam. You could know all the answers, but if you run out of time, you won't finish. Most 11+ papers give you 45–60 minutes to answer 20–40 questions. This guide teaches you exactly how to allocate your time, pace yourself, and make strategic decisions about which questions to spend time on and which to skip.

The Four-Phase Exam Strategy

The Four Phases of Exam Time Management
Plan your exam in four phases: Scan (2 min) → Easy Questions (phase 1) → Medium Questions (phase 2) → Hard Questions & Check (phase 3–4).

Phase 1: First 2 Minutes — Scan & Plan (Not Solving)

What to Do:

  • Read the entire paper WITHOUT solving anything
  • Identify how many questions there are
  • Spot which questions look easiest (you'll do these first)
  • Spot which questions look hardest (you'll do these last or skip)
  • Note any special instructions (e.g., "multiple answers possible")
  • Identify the time you expect to finish each section
Example:

You have a 45-minute Maths paper with 30 questions.
Scan time: 2 minutes
Solving time: 40 minutes (75 seconds × 30 questions ÷ 60 = 37.5 min)
Buffer time: 3 minutes

You notice: Questions 1–10 are arithmetic (easy), 11–20 are mixed (medium), 21–30 are word problems (hard).
Plan: Q1–10 (15 min), Q11–20 (18 min), Q21–25 only (10 min), Q26–30 skip unless time, check answers (2 min).

Phase 2: Easy Questions First — Build Confidence & Points (Minutes 2–20)

What to Do:

  • Tackle the questions you identified as easy during the scan
  • Spend your allocated time (don't overspend on easy questions)
  • Write clearly — one correct answer you marked is worth more than three you didn't write down
  • Check your answer is reasonable (e.g., a percentage should be 0–100%)
  • Move on — don't get stuck on an easy question for 5 minutes
Common Mistake:

Students spend 3 minutes on Question 1 "to get it perfect", then have to rush through the harder questions. Wrong strategy. You want to get as many questions right as possible, not get fewer questions perfectly right.

How to Avoid Getting Stuck: Set a personal time limit for each question. For a 45-minute paper with 30 questions, spend maximum 2 minutes on an "easy" question. If stuck after 2 minutes, mark it and move on.

Phase 3: Medium Questions — Push Yourself (Minutes 20–35)

What to Do:

  • Now tackle the medium-difficulty questions you identified
  • Spend 2–3 minutes per question (they're harder, so take a bit longer)
  • Show your working — this way, even if the answer is wrong, you get partial credit (where applicable)
  • If stuck after 2–3 minutes: mark a guess and move on
  • Don't go back to earlier questions yet
Example:

Question 15: "A rectangle has a perimeter of 24 cm. The length is 8 cm. What is the width?"
You work it out: 2L + 2W = 24, so 2(8) + 2W = 24, so 16 + 2W = 24, so 2W = 8, so W = 4.
Write: "Perimeter = 24 cm. L = 8 cm. 2L + 2W = 24. 16 + 2W = 24. 2W = 8. W = 4 cm."
Check: Does 4 cm make sense for the width? Yes (width is less than length).
Move on — don't second-guess yourself.

Phase 4: Hard Questions & Check (Minutes 35–45)

What to Do:

  • You have ~10 minutes left
  • Spend 1 minute on each hard question (write a reasonable guess)
  • Once you've "attempted" all questions (even with guesses), use remaining time to check
  • Check your easier answers first (you're more likely to fix simple careless errors)
  • If you spot an error, fix it
  • If you're not sure about a fix, leave it (your first answer is often right)
Critical Rule:

Leave NO blank answers. Even a guess has a 20% chance (on a 5-option multiple choice). A blank is 0%.

Time Allocation by Question Difficulty

How to Allocate Time by Question Difficulty
Spend 60 seconds on easy questions, 90–120 on medium, and only 60 seconds on hard (guess if stuck). This maximizes your score.

Exam Day Pace Strategies

Strategy 1: The "Easy First" Approach (Recommended for Most Students)

How It Works:

Do all easy questions, then medium, then hard. This maximises points because you don't waste time on hard questions you might not answer correctly anyway.

  • Phase 1: All questions worth 1 point (or easy)
  • Phase 2: All questions worth 2–3 points (medium)
  • Phase 3: All questions worth 4+ points (hard)
  • Result: You've definitely earned most points by skipping hard questions

Strategy 2: The "Straight Through" Approach (For Fast Finishers)

How It Works:

Answer questions in order (Q1, Q2, Q3...). Spend your time limit on each, then move on. No skipping and coming back.

  • Spend 60–90 seconds on each question
  • If stuck, write a guess and move on immediately
  • You'll reach the end with time for a full check
  • Result: Complete paper, time for checking

Strategy 3: The "Medium First" Approach (For Uncertain Students)

How It Works:

Skip the very hard-looking questions. Do easy and medium, then go back to hard with whatever time is left.

  • Phase 1: All questions that look manageable
  • Phase 2: Go back to hard questions only if time remains
  • Result: You don't waste 10 minutes on one impossible question
Which Strategy for You?
• Fast finisher who rarely gets stuck? Strategy 2 (Straight Through)
• Confident but methodical? Strategy 1 (Easy First)
• Prone to panic on hard questions? Strategy 3 (Medium First)

Handling Specific Challenges

Challenge 1: You Get Stuck on a Question

❌ Wrong Approach

Spend 5+ minutes trying to figure it out. Get stressed. Rush the rest of the paper.

✓ Correct Approach

Step 1 (30 sec): Read the question twice.
Step 2 (30 sec): Try a method. If it's not working, stop.
Step 3 (30 sec): Write your best guess (don't leave blank).
Step 4: Mark it with a star and MOVE ON immediately.

Challenge 2: You're Running Out of Time

❌ Wrong Approach

Panic. Rush your answers. Leave questions blank. Write messy.

✓ Correct Approach

If 10 minutes left: You can do ~7 more questions. Speed up, but keep writing.
If 5 minutes left: Quick guesses on remaining questions. Don't leave blanks.
If 1 minute left: Circle your best guesses on any remaining blanks. Legibility matters more than accuracy now.

Challenge 3: You Finish Early

❌ Wrong Approach

Submit your paper. You've "finished early so I must be smart."

✓ Correct Approach

Step 1: Check your easiest answers (Q1–10). Look for careless errors.
Step 2: Check your working for Maths questions. Did you drop a zero? Wrong operation?
Step 3: Reread tricky Verbal Reasoning questions. Did you read the full question?
Don't change answers unless you're sure the new one is right.

Time Management Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Spending Too Long on Difficult Questions

A student spends 8 minutes on a hard question worth 1 mark, gets it wrong, then rushes the remaining 10 easy questions and gets 4 of them wrong. Net result: −6 marks. Instead, they should have skipped the hard one and gotten all 10 easy ones right: +10 marks.

Mistake 2: Not Leaving Buffer Time

Student finishes answering all questions at 44 minutes out of 45. No time to check. They spot a silly mistake in Question 2 (forgot to read "double the answer") but can't fix it.

Mistake 3: Changing Answers in the Final Check

During the final 5 minutes, student second-guesses their answer to Question 5 and changes it. The new answer is wrong. The original was right. Net loss of a mark.

Mistake 4: Not Writing a Guess

Student doesn't understand Question 15. Leaves it blank. If they'd written their best guess, they'd have a 20% chance on multiple choice (1 in 5). Blank is 0%.

Mistake 5: Rushing Your Handwriting

Student rushes in the final 10 minutes. Their answer "41" looks like "4l" or "14". Marked wrong. If they'd spent 5 more seconds writing clearly, they'd have gotten it right.

Practice: Simulate Exam Conditions

How to Practice Time Management:

  • Take full practice papers under timed conditions — not at your own pace
  • Use a timer (your phone's stopwatch works)
  • Don't pause the timer to get a drink or go to the toilet
  • After each paper, note: (a) How many you finished? (b) How many you got right? (c) Were you too slow, too fast, or just right?
  • If too slow, identify which questions made you slow. Was it hard questions? Careless errors? Difficulty reading?
  • Next paper: Apply the strategy that fits your weakness
Practice Example:

Paper 1 (untimed): 30 questions, got 24 right (80%)
Paper 2 (timed 45 min): 30 questions, got 20 right (67%)
Analysis: You lost 4 marks due to time pressure. Likely cause: You spent too long on hard questions or didn't leave buffer time to check.
Strategy for Paper 3: Use "Easy First" approach. Do Q1–15 in first 15 minutes (easy questions). Do Q16–25 in next 12 minutes (medium). Attempt Q26–30 in 8 minutes. Check in final 10 minutes.

The Golden Rule: Speed vs. Accuracy

Which matters more: Speed or Accuracy?
Answer: Speed, up to a point.

A student who answers 30 questions and gets 25 right (83%) beats a student who answers 20 questions and gets 18 right (90%). First student: 25 marks. Second student: 18 marks.

This is why time management is critical. You'd rather get 80% right on all questions than 90% right on half the questions.

Summary: Your Time Management Plan

  1. Before the exam: Know which exam board and paper type you're taking. Look up the exact time limit and number of questions.
  2. On exam day: Spend 2 minutes scanning the paper. Identify easy, medium, hard questions.
  3. Phase 1 (Easy questions): Do all easy questions first. Spend 30–60 seconds each. Build confidence.
  4. Phase 2 (Medium questions): Do medium questions. Spend 90–120 seconds each. Show working.
  5. Phase 3 (Hard questions): Spend 60–90 seconds each. Write reasonable guesses. Don't leave blanks.
  6. Phase 4 (Check & Fix): Use final 5–10 minutes to check your easiest answers. Fix careless errors only.
  7. After the exam: Review how your time management went. What slowed you down? Improve for the next paper.
