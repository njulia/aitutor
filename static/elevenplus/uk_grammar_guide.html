A Parent's Guide to the 11+

Grammar schools in England and Northern Ireland select pupils through an entrance exam sat in Year 6 — but the subjects tested, the timing, and the exam provider all vary by area and even by individual school. There is no single national format.

This guide sets out what's usually tested, a sensible term-by-term timeline for preparation, and how to read school rankings without being misled by them.

What's Actually Tested

Most 11+ exams draw on some combination of four subjects, split across two or three papers. Not every school tests all four — some drop English entirely, others add extras.

Mathematics

KS2 arithmetic and reasoning, stretched into Year 6 topics: ratio, algebra basics, geometry, data handling.

English

Comprehension, spelling, punctuation, grammar and vocabulary. Around 30% of grammar schools skip this subject.

Verbal Reasoning

Word-based logic: codes, analogies, hidden words — drawn from a bank of around 21 named question types.

Non-Verbal Reasoning

Pattern and shape logic — series, matrices, rotations. Tests reasoning independent of language or maths knowledge.

Some schools also test:

+ Creative Writing + Verbal Skills + Science + 3D Non-Verbal Reasoning

A Preparation Timeline

Most families begin preparing in Year 4 or early Year 5 — a little and often, rather than a last-minute cram. Exact dates depend entirely on your local authority or consortium, so treat this as a shape, not a schedule.

Year 4 — Spring & Summer

Build the foundations

  • Strengthen daily reading and times-tables fluency — no exam-style practice yet
  • Introduce the idea of the 11+ casually, so it doesn't arrive as a shock
  • Start noticing which local schools use which exam board (check admissions pages)

Year 5 — Autumn Term

First exposure to reasoning

  • Introduce Verbal and Non-Verbal Reasoning — most children haven't met this style before
  • Short, low-pressure sessions: 20–30 minutes, a few times a week
  • Confirm your target schools' subjects, exam provider and registration process

Year 5 — Spring & Summer

Structured practice

  • Move to timed practice papers across all tested subjects
  • Register for the local authority test or consortium (deadlines are often in the summer term — check early)
  • Start reviewing wrong answers together, not just marking them

Year 6 — September Onward

Exam window

  • Many consortia sit the test in September–November; some areas differ — confirm your exact date
  • Final full-length timed mocks under exam conditions
  • Focus shifts to technique and pacing rather than new content

Year 6 — March

National Offer Day

  • Secondary school offers are released on National Offer Day (1 March, or the next working day)
  • An appeals process exists if a place isn't offered — check your local authority's timetable

How Grammar School Rankings Work

League tables (The Times Parent Power, DfE performance tables, and others) rank schools mainly on GCSE grades at 9–7, A-level grades at A*–A, and Progress 8 scores.

Read with care: exact numbered positions vary by publisher, year and methodology — a school ranked 4th in one table may sit 9th in another. Grammar schools also select already-able pupils, which naturally lifts results regardless of teaching quality. Treat rankings as one input, not a verdict — fit, pastoral care and commute matter just as much.

Exam Providers Differ Too

GL Assessment

Used by the majority of consortia. Typically multiple-choice with five answer options, drawing on a documented, publicly-listed set of question types across Maths, English, VR and NVR.

CEM / CSSE-style

Some areas use CEM-style or their own bespoke tests instead, which can blend subjects within a single paper. Providers and formats do change over time — always confirm directly with your target school or local authority.
