English Comprehension Strategies for the 11+

Master the skills needed to excel at reading comprehension questions in the 11+ exam.

Practice Comprehension Questions

Understanding the Comprehension Section

The 11+ English comprehension section tests your ability to understand written passages, extract information, and make inferences. Most papers include 2-3 passages with 15-25 questions each.

Key Reading Strategies

1. Skim Before You Read Deeply

First, quickly read through the passage to understand the main idea and structure. This takes only 1-2 minutes but gives you crucial context.

2. Identify the Main Idea

Ask yourself: What is this passage mainly about? What is the author's main point? This helps you understand the purpose and tone.

3. Annotate as You Read

Underline or highlight key information, circle difficult words, and mark where the main points are explained.

💡 Tip: Don't annotate everything - focus on names, numbers, key events, and turning points in the narrative.

Types of Comprehension Questions

Literal Questions

These ask for information directly stated in the text. Look for the exact words or phrases in the passage.

Inferential Questions

These require you to read between the lines. You must combine information from the text with your own knowledge to draw conclusions.

Vocabulary in Context

Find the word in the passage and use the surrounding sentences to determine its meaning. Don't rely on definitions you already know.

Author's Purpose & Tone

Consider why the author wrote this and what feeling or attitude they express. Look for words that show emotion or opinion.

Time Management for Comprehension

Typical timing for a 50-minute paper:

  • Read passage 1: 3-4 minutes
  • Answer questions 1-25: 10-12 minutes
  • Read passage 2: 3-4 minutes
  • Answer questions 26-50: 10-12 minutes
  • Review and check: 10 minutes
⏱️ Strategy: Don't spend more than 2 minutes on any single question. If stuck, move on and come back to it later.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Reading too slowly: You don't need to understand every single word - focus on main ideas
  • Skipping the passage: Always read the passage first, don't jump to questions
  • Relying on assumptions: Stick to what the text actually says
  • Missing key phrases: Words like "not," "however," and "but" can completely change meaning
  • Overthinking: The answer is usually in the passage - don't create complex interpretations

Practice Tips

Work through past papers and practice passages regularly. Time yourself to build speed and accuracy. After completing practice passages, review your mistakes carefully to identify patterns in what you struggle with.
