11+ Pass Mark & Standardised Scores: What Score Does Your Child Need?

Updated August 2026 · UK 11+ preparation guide

Important: 11+ arrangements vary by school and region. Always check the current admissions and test information published by the school, consortium or local authority you are applying to.

One of the most common questions parents ask is: what is a good 11+ score? The answer depends on the school, test provider, cohort and admissions rules. There is no single national 11+ pass mark for every grammar school.

Raw score vs standardised score

A raw score is simply the number of questions answered correctly. A standardised score converts performance onto a common scale and can take account of factors such as the difficulty of the paper and the child's age. This is why a practice score such as 32 out of 40 should not automatically be treated as a universal pass or fail.

Why pass marks vary

Selective schools and consortia can use different tests, weighting and admission arrangements. Some publish a qualifying threshold; others use ranking and oversubscription rules. Always check the current admissions information for the schools you are considering.

How to use practice scores

  • Track accuracy by subject and question type.
  • Look for improvement across several papers rather than one score.
  • Review mistakes and practise the underlying skill.
  • Use the target school's current admissions information for the final benchmark.

What should parents do?

Use mock exams to spot trends, not to predict a place with certainty. A child who is improving steadily and can work accurately under time pressure is building useful exam readiness.
